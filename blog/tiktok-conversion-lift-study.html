How TikTok's Conversion Lift Study works

How TikTok's managed Conversion Lift Study splits your audience, what data it needs, how to read its lift metrics, and where Unified Lift fits.

Chelsea Cramer · Nov 17, 2026

Incrementality testing by platform: Overview · Meta · Google · TikTok · Amazon · Pinterest · Snapchat

TikTok's Conversion Lift Study is a managed experiment: TikTok randomly splits people into a test group that sees your ads and a control group that doesn't, then measures the extra conversions your campaign drove. You can't launch it yourself. You go through a TikTok representative.

The idea in one picture

You supply the conversion data. TikTok handles the split and the analysis.

How a study runs

  1. Groups. TikTok sets up a randomized test group and control group. Both keep receiving your other marketing.
  2. Data. You share conversion data from the study period. Sources are the Events API (recommended), TikTok Pixel, mobile measurement partners, or TikTok Shop, which measures on-platform purchases automatically.
  3. Comparison. TikTok compares conversions in the two groups over time.
  4. Readout. TikTok's measurement experts deliver an analysis with findings and recommendations.

Account representatives help define objectives, key metrics, duration and the test and control groups. A study can cover one campaign or all your TikTok ads (TikTok Help Center).

What you need

  • An eligible account. TikTok describes the study as a managed service and sends you to your account rep to start.
  • Minimum ad spend based on your objective and study scope. TikTok doesn't publish the amounts.
  • A supported conversion goal: website purchases, app installs or offline sales.
  • A campaign that runs long enough to produce statistically significant data. TikTok doesn't state a length.
  • Conversion data you can share with TikTok.

What you get back

MetricWhat it means
Incremental conversionsExtra conversions your ads drove
Absolute liftDifference between test and control (see note below)
Relative liftThat difference as a share of the control rate (see note below)
CPICCost per incremental conversion
iROASIncremental revenue per dollar spent
Confidence levelHow sure you can be the ads caused the lift

Note: TikTok's help page defines absolute lift as a percentage and relative lift as the rate difference as a share of control. That reverses common usage, as PPC Land points out. Confirm definitions with your rep before comparing against other platforms.

Unified Lift: brand and conversion lift in one study

TikTok also sells Unified Lift, which runs a Brand Lift Study and a Conversion Lift Study together so you see both perception and sales from one test. TikTok introduced it at TikTok World 2024 (TikTok Newsroom). Torrid and its agency Ovative used one to read brand perception and incremental sales from a full-funnel TikTok plan (TikTok case study). Like the conversion study, TikTok's measurement team sets it up.

What TikTok says its studies show

These are claims from TikTok's own marketing page, not independent findings:

  • More than 1,400 global advertisers have run a study.
  • A majority of studies in the past year showed significant incremental value.
  • A 2024 TransUnion meta-analysis found 52% of TikTok-led incremental conversions were exclusive to TikTok reach.
  • One fashion retailer found TikTok drove 2.4 times the conversions its last-click model showed, and its overall ROI rose 33% after it moved budget.

Geo tests

TikTok's own pages describe only the randomized test-and-control design. PPC Land reported on April 17, 2024 that TikTok also sells geo lift tests, which raise spend in selected regions for a few weeks (PPC Land).

Watch-outs

  • TikTok designs, runs and reads out its own study.
  • App conversions count opted-in users only, according to PPC Land.
  • The study measures TikTok alone, so it can't show how TikTok interacts with other channels.
  • TikTok says a study can help you check your attribution window, which makes it a useful calibration tool.
