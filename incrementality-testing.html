Why incrementality testing matters

Every ad platform reports its own results, and every one of them over-credits itself. Incrementality testing answers a simpler question: what would have happened without this spend? By holding out a group of people or markets and comparing them to a group that saw the ads, you measure the lift a channel truly causes, not just the conversions it touched.

Brands usually bring me in when they want to:

  • Prove whether a big channel, like paid social, search or TV, is actually driving new revenue
  • Validate or calibrate a marketing mix model
  • Measure channels that platform attribution can't see, like CTV, audio, OOH and brand campaigns
  • Build a regular testing program instead of one-off tests

How I design incrementality tests

  • Geo experiments and geo-lift. Turn spend up, down or off in matched markets. Works for nearly any channel, including offline, and doesn't depend on user-level tracking.
  • Holdout and conversion lift tests. Use platform tools or audience holdouts where they're reliable, with an independent read on the results.
  • Synthetic controls and diff-in-diff. When a clean randomized test isn't possible, these build a credible comparison from historical data.
  • Power and duration planning. Every test is sized before it launches, so you know it can detect the effect you care about and you don't burn budget on inconclusive results.
  • Readouts leadership can use. Each test ends with a clear call on what to scale, cut or test next.

Common questions

FAQ

What is incrementality testing?

Incrementality testing measures the extra sales or sign-ups a marketing channel actually causes, by comparing a group exposed to the marketing with a similar group that wasn't. It separates true lift from conversions that would have happened anyway.

What is a geo experiment?

A geo experiment changes spend in some geographic markets while leaving matched markets unchanged, then compares results. Because it works on aggregate market data, it can measure channels like TV and CTV and isn't affected by cookie or tracking loss.

How long does an incrementality test take?

Most tests run between two and eight weeks, depending on your sales volume, the size of the expected effect and the channel's lag. I size each test up front so it runs long enough to give a clear answer.

Can we trust the lift studies ad platforms offer?

They can be useful, but the platform is grading its own work and controls the method. I use them where they're sound and add independent geo tests to check the results.

How does incrementality testing work with MMM?

They work best together. Experiments give precise, causal reads on specific channels, and the marketing mix model covers everything at once. Calibrating the MMM with test results makes the whole picture more accurate.
